Studio Policy & Tuition Kit for Private Music Teachers

Fill-in policies, tuition math and word-for-word parent scripts. Works for any instrument or voice and any currency (examples use EUR). This is a business template, not legal advice: check your local consumer, contract and tax rules before publishing your policy.

---

1. Make These 5 Decisions First

Almost every studio headache (missed lessons, late payments, awkward refund requests) comes from one of five undecided questions. Decide them once, in this order.

| # | Decision | Recommended default | Choose differently if... |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Billing model | Flat monthly tuition, billed in advance | You teach mostly adults with irregular schedules: sell 5- or 10-lesson packages that expire after 8 or 16 weeks |
| 2 | Missed lessons | Teacher cancels: always made up. Student cancels 24h+ ahead: 1 makeup per term from fixed makeup slots. Under 24h or no-show: forfeited | You have a waiting list: drop teacher-run makeups and use a swap list |
| 3 | Payment due date | 1st of the month, late after the 5th | Most families are paid biweekly: due the 1st, late after the 10th |
| 4 | Late fee | EUR 10 flat after the grace period; lessons pause after 14 days unpaid | Fees feel hostile in your community: no fee, but autopay required |
| 5 | Leaving the studio | 30 days written notice; notice period billed in full | Summer-only students: notice waived for summer enrollment |

Decision rule: if you lose more than 2 paid teaching hours per month to cancellations, switch to flat monthly tuition before changing anything else. That single change fixes most of the other problems.

2. Tuition Math

2.1 Set your hourly target from income, not from competitors

Worksheet:

  • Target gross annual income from teaching: ______ (A)
  • Annual business costs (instrument maintenance, books, software, travel, insurance, association fees, training): ______ (B)
  • Teaching weeks per year (typically 36 to 46): ______ (C)
  • Teaching hours per week you can sustain: ______ (D)
  • Required rate per teaching hour = (A + B) / (C x D)

Worked example: A = 30,000, B = 3,000, C = 40 weeks, D = 22 hours.

33,000 / 880 = EUR 37.50 per hour.

| Lesson length | Rate |
|---|---|
| 30 min | EUR 19 (18.75 rounded) |
| 45 min | EUR 28 |
| 60 min | EUR 38 |

Reality check for empty slots: expect 10 to 15% of slots to sit empty at any time because of churn. If your schedule is rarely full, divide by (C x D x 0.85) instead. In the example: 33,000 / (40 x 18.7) = EUR 44.12 per hour.

Admin time: expect roughly 1 unpaid hour of prep, messaging and recital work for every 4 teaching hours. Because D counts only teaching hours, this is already priced in. Do not add it a second time.

Market check: if your number is more than 20% above the local median, either make the difference visible (credentials, exam results, recitals, dedicated studio) or reduce B / increase C before you lower the rate.

2.2 Convert per-lesson rates to flat monthly tuition

Formula: Monthly tuition = (lessons per year x per-lesson rate) / billing months

Billing months: 12 (smoothest for family budgets), 10 (September to June, summer separate) or 9.

36 lessons per year (typical school-year calendar):

| Per-lesson rate | Annual | / 12 | / 10 | / 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EUR 20 | 720 | 60 | 72 | 80 |
| EUR 25 | 900 | 75 | 90 | 100 |
| EUR 30 | 1,080 | 90 | 108 | 120 |
| EUR 35 | 1,260 | 105 | 126 | 140 |
| EUR 40 | 1,440 | 120 | 144 | 160 |

44 lessons per year (year-round with breaks), billed over 12 months:

| Per-lesson rate | Annual | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| EUR 20 | 880 | 73 |
| EUR 25 | 1,100 | 92 |
| EUR 30 | 1,320 | 110 |
| EUR 35 | 1,540 | 128 |
| EUR 40 | 1,760 | 147 |

Rule: publish the number of lessons per year in your policy (for example: Tuition covers 36 lessons between September and June). A month with 5 lesson weeks and a month with 3 cost the same. The published annual count is what you owe, not the weekly count.

Rounding: round to a whole number. If you want cleaner figures, round to the nearest 5 and adjust the published lesson count by one if needed.

2.3 Discounts that will not hurt you

  • Sibling discount: 5 to 10% off the second and further students, never the first.
  • Pay-the-term-upfront discount: 3 to 5%. The cash flow is worth it.
  • Never discount because a family will miss a few weeks. Holding a slot costs full tuition.

2.4 Summer options

  • (a) Included: 12-month billing covers a stated number of summer lessons.
  • (b) Summer package: choose 4, 6 or 8 lessons scheduled flexibly, paid by June 15.
  • (c) Slot-hold fee: 50% of monthly tuition keeps the September slot without summer lessons.

Default: if you have a waiting list, use (a) or (c). If not, (b) brings in income without forcing anyone.

3. Missed Lesson Policy

Pick one model and paste its text into your policy.

Model A: Fixed makeup slots (best for most studios)

> I offer [2-4] makeup slots each month. Students who cancel at least 24 hours in advance may book one makeup lesson per term into an available slot. Unused makeups expire at the end of the term. Tuition is not credited or refunded for lessons missed by the student.

Model B: Swap list (best for full studios)

> Families may join the studio swap list (first name, contact and lesson time). If you cannot attend, you may arrange a swap with another family and let me know at least 24 hours in advance. I do not schedule makeup lessons for student absences.

Model C: Built-in makeup weeks

> Tuition covers [36] lessons scheduled across [38] weeks. Weeks [19] and [38] are makeup weeks. Lessons cancelled by me and absences notified at least 24 hours in advance are rescheduled into these weeks. Other missed lessons are forfeited.

Universal rules, whatever model you choose:

  • Teacher cancellations are always made up or credited. This is what makes the strict rules feel fair.
  • Student unwell: offer an online lesson at the regular time. It counts as delivered.
  • Weather: if you close, it is a teacher cancellation. If the family chooses not to travel, offer online.
  • Makeups never roll over between terms.
  • A missed makeup is never made up again.
  • Holidays and closure dates are listed at enrollment and already excluded from the lesson count.

6. Parent and Student Scripts

6.1 Late payment sequence

Day 6 (friendly):

> Hi [Name], a quick reminder that [Student]'s tuition for [Month] (EUR ___) hasn't come through yet. As per the studio policy a EUR 10 late fee applies from today, so the total is now EUR ___. Payment details: [link]. Thanks!

Day 10:

> Hi [Name], following up on [Month] tuition (EUR ___ including the late fee). If something has come up, reply and we can agree a date this week. Lessons pause on the 14th if the account is still open.

Day 14:

> Hi [Name], as tuition for [Month] is still outstanding, I'm pausing [Student]'s lessons from today. I'm holding the [Day, time] slot until [date 7 days out]; after that I'll need to offer it to the waiting list. As soon as payment arrives we'll pick up at the next lesson.

Decision rule: waive the late fee once per family per year if they ask politely. Never waive the pause.

6.2 Cancellation under 24 hours

> Thanks for letting me know, I hope [Student] feels better soon. Because this is less than 24 hours before the lesson it falls outside the makeup window in our policy, but we can do today's lesson online at the usual time if [Student] is up to it. Otherwise, see you next [day]!

6.3 Refund request for missed lessons

> I understand, it's frustrating to miss lessons. Tuition reserves [Student]'s weekly time and isn't refundable for lessons missed by the student, but here's what I can offer: [a makeup slot on X / an online lesson / written feedback on a practice video]. Would one of those work?

6.4 We are away for 3 weeks, can we pause?

> Your slot stays reserved while you're away, so tuition continues as normal. To make the most of it, I can send a practice plan for the trip, or we can schedule one online lesson while you're away. Which would help more?

Optional flexible add-on:

> If you'd prefer, you can release the slot and rejoin when a space opens, but I can't guarantee the same time.

6.5 Rate increase announcement

> Subject: [Studio Name] tuition for [Season]

>

> Dear families,

>

> Thank you for a wonderful year. [One specific highlight: the spring recital, exam results, a festival.]

>

> From [date at least 60 days away], monthly tuition will be:

> - 30-minute lessons: EUR ___ (currently EUR ___)

> - 45-minute lessons: EUR ___ (currently EUR ___)

> - 60-minute lessons: EUR ___ (currently EUR ___)

>

> This reflects rising costs for [materials, studio rent, training] and lets me keep investing in [specific thing]. Everything else in the studio policy stays the same.

>

> The updated policy is attached. Please reply to confirm [Student]'s place for [Season] by [date].

>

> Warm regards, [Name]

One sentence of reasons is enough. Do not apologise.

6.6 Switching current families from pay-per-lesson to monthly

> Dear families,

>

> Starting [September 1], [Studio] is moving to monthly tuition. Instead of paying per lesson, you'll pay the same amount each month, covering [36] lessons from September to June.

>

> What this means for you:

> - Same yearly cost: [36] lessons at EUR ___ = EUR ___, spread over [10] payments of EUR ___.

> - A guaranteed weekly slot, reserved for you all year.

> - One makeup lesson per term when you give 24 hours notice.

> - No more counting weeks or settling up at each lesson.

>

> Please confirm your slot by [date]. Slots not confirmed by then will be offered to the waiting list.

6.7 Student wants to quit

> Thanks for telling me, I appreciate it. Under the studio policy there's a 30-day notice period, so [Student]'s last lesson would be [date] and the final tuition payment is for [Month]. Before you decide: sometimes a few weeks of different repertoire or a break from exam pieces changes everything. Happy to try that, and happy to support whatever you choose.

6.8 Adult student who cancels repeatedly

> Hi [Name], I've noticed [Day] has been tricky lately (3 of the last 6 lessons). Would a different slot, a 10-lesson package or every-other-week lessons suit your schedule better? Happy to look at options.

7. Rate Increase Rules

  • Review once a year, in the same month every year.
  • Size: 3 to 8%. Under 3% is not worth the admin; over 10% triggers comparison shopping. If you are more than 15% below your target from 2.1, close the gap over two years.
  • Notice: minimum 60 days; 90 days if you run on a school year.
  • Timing: announce in late spring for the new season. Never mid-term.
  • New students pay the new rate immediately; update your website first.
  • Grandfathering: only for a fixed period (one term at most), never indefinitely.
  • Expect 0 to 5% of families to leave after a modest increase and budget for it.

9. Rollout Checklist for Changing Policy in an Existing Studio

  • [ ] Five decisions from section 1 made and written down
  • [ ] Hourly rate and monthly tuition calculated
  • [ ] Policy drafted from the template and fits on one page
  • [ ] Closure dates listed and lesson count verified against the calendar
  • [ ] Payment method set up (autopay preferred)
  • [ ] Announcement (6.6 or 6.5) sent at least 60 days before the effective date
  • [ ] Re-enrollment form with signature line sent
  • [ ] Reminder sent 7 days before the deadline
  • [ ] Waiting list contacted for unconfirmed slots
  • [ ] First month: every rule enforced consistently. Your first exception becomes your real policy.

10. Six Mistakes to Avoid

  • Prorating months. Always bill the published monthly amount.
  • Letting makeup credits accumulate. Cap at one per term.
  • Enforcing rules for some families but not others. Word travels.
  • Discussing money at the door. Use messages and the scripts above.
  • Writing a policy longer than one page. Nobody reads page two.
  • Raising rates mid-season. Change prices only at the season start.
